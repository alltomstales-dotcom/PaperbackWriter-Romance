# Writer brief (what each drafting agent gets)

Every drafting agent gets `WRITER_BRIEF.md`, the skeleton for its chapters, `canon.md`, the cast, and the previous chapters. The brief tells it:

1. **POV is in the skeleton.** Write from inside that lead, close third, past tense. Don't head-hop inside a scene.
2. **Each chapter does the job the skeleton names.** It carries its beat, if it has one, and its spark.
3. **A spark is an exchange.** Something passes between them, in what they say or do, that changes the air. It is not a description of eyes or hands.
4. **Planted lines stay plain.** A beat's line sits inside a scene about something else. Say it once, in passing, and don't decorate it. Writers obey hiding rules and then embellish; the embellishment is what a reader notices.
5. **No narrator winks.** Never write "she didn't know yet", "later she would remember", "the right answer", "something about it nagged at her", or any sentence that tells the reader what will matter.
6. **Sweet heat.** Kissing on the page. Anything more happens off the page, if at all. `heat` checks every chapter.
7. **The period.** Nothing in `BANNED.txt`: no modern idioms or objects that don't exist yet. A real fact you lean on is either in `canon.md` or you log it.
8. **Canon wins.** Use only names, dates, sums and distances from `canon.md`. Anything you invent goes in `invented.md`, with the chapter.
9. **Length.** 2,000 to 2,500 words a chapter, unless the skeleton says otherwise.
10. **One chapter per Write call,** to `chapters/chNN.txt`, starting `Chapter N: Title`. Never two chapters in one call.
11. **Self-check before you stop:** POV held, beat placed plainly, a spark, no winks, nothing past a kiss, nothing banned, inventions logged.
